When you start the conversation

“Help me make sense of this.”

  • “What changed in my bloodwork?”Bring in your results and explore the trends.
  • “I’m travelling next week.”Prepare a plan around your routine and medication.
  • “Get my new doctor up to speed.”Review a health brief before you share it.

When Baymax starts the check-in

“Before you get on with your day…”

  • How’s your energy?A quick check-in gives today’s plan some context.
  • Water? Movement? A proper break?Your everyday goals stay in view.
  • What’s still on your list?Keep the next small step close at hand.

In-app check-ins work today. Background push, SMS and server-sent email are next steps.

Your records have
a longer story to tell.

One blood test is a snapshot. Put results together and you can see what’s changing, what’s holding steady, and what to ask your clinician.

Attach a text-based PDF or a supported text file in chat. Baymax can read it with you and chart available lab values across results.

PDFs need selectable text. Scans and photos aren’t supported yet.

  • Conversation, records & lab chartsAgent responses, document uploads and biomarker trends.

  • Apple Health, via ShortcutRun an iPhone Shortcut to send steps, exercise, sleep and water.

  • Installable web appAdd Baymax to your phone’s home screen.

Still a demo

Pharmacy prices, stock and checkout are sample flows. No payment is taken and no order is placed.

Still on the list

Background push delivery, SMS, server-sent email, account sign-in and cross-device access.

The walkthroughs use scripted sample responses to show the interface. They aren’t recordings of live clinical advice.

A note on health data and privacy

This is a synthetic-data prototype. Real health data and clinical use need further security, privacy and compliance review; HIPAA compliance has not been verified. The app includes optional workspace memory and deletion controls. Demo uploads and records are stored separately from that setting.

Baymax helps organise information and prepare questions. It doesn’t diagnose, prescribe, change treatment or provide emergency care.
